The Path to 50% Wealth Penetration

The current FRP lands penetration at 42.8% by 2030 — 7.2pp short of the ambition. A portfolio-system diagnosis of why, and the interventions that close the gap.

We are not on track for 50% — the plan itself only reaches 42.8% by 2030. The issue is not one conversion campaign; it is a portfolio system problem across acquisition quality, on-book conversion, and structural dilution. We run a funnel-diagnostic loop — define the outcome, diagnose the funnel, isolate the true bottleneck, intervene, close the measurement loop — borrowing the operating instinct behind dialer.io: relentlessly diagnose where the funnel leaks, and fix the true bottleneck first.

Portfolio dilution diagnostic · value protection

What is structurally dragging penetration down?

Sources of structural dilution

  • ▸ Customers below the qualification threshold
  • ▸ Grace-period & persistently inactive Premier customers
  • ▸ Re-upgraded customers with no incremental value
  • ▸ P1PA / multi-market customers with unclear qualification
  • ▸ Low-value customers consuming RM capacity
  • ▸ Upgrade–downgrade recyclers
  • ▸ Markets improving penetration only by denominator reduction

Data flags: CIIOM swings 0%→18%→14%→20% and TRB-qual jumps 38%→51%→42% — classic denominator noise. US penetration is stuck at ~17–23% despite a 300k+ book. Revenue per customer is flat (2,022→2,025) while TRB per customer rises — value isn't converting to revenue.

A simple portfolio classification

Grow

Clear upside; invest.

Convert

Credible near-term path.

Retain

Valuable; protect.

Migrate

Right-fit service model.

Downgrade

Persistently unqualified.

Exit / inactive

No credible path.

Primary question: which customers have a credible path to value, and which are permanently diluting the portfolio?

Isolate the true bottleneck

The true bottleneck is acquisition quality

Three stages leak, but they are not equal. On-book conversion and clean-up move the ratio at the margin; the largest and most persistent leak is low-quality inflow entering at roughly half the on-book wealth rate and diluting the mix every year.

MOB3 qualification flat at ~38–39% since 2024; TRB per new customer dipped in 2025. Every vintage acquired at 22% pulls the on-book average down before conversion can lift it.

Rank the levers by leverage

Primary lever NTB · acquisition quality

Largest, most persistent drag. Fix first — raise the MOB3 line toward on-book, or the gap never closes.

Accelerant ETB · on-book conversion

Real, provable momentum (NNIA >200% vs PY) — but it cannot outrun continuous low-quality inflow on its own.

Guardrail Clean-up · dilution

Protects the ratio and RM capacity — but cannot be the primary route to 50%.

Move the MOB3 line toward the on-book line and the 2030 gap closes. Keep acquiring at 22%, and no amount of on-book conversion catches up.

NTB action plan · the isolated bottleneck

Make acquisition the first quality-control point

1 · Define "good NTB"

An account opened is not "good". A good NTB customer is:

  • funded within X days
  • TRB qualified within Y days
  • generates NNIA within Z days
  • stays qualified past early life

Target: lift MOB3 penetration from 22% toward the on-book 36%.

2 · Quality gates

Apply the definition by:

  • market · channel · campaign
  • proposition · product hook
  • customer source

3 · Separate acquisition from conversion

Track distinctly:

  • acquired · funded · qualified
  • wealth activated
  • NNIA producing · sustained

4 · Reallocate acquisition investment

  • Scale high-quality channels (AOC-type, MOB3 pen. 45%)
  • Redesign weak channels (UAE / SGH MOB3 pen. ~10%)
  • Stop persistently dilutive acquisition

5 · Build hero conversion pathways

  • TD hook → income / yield solution
  • International (UAE salary credits) → offshore wealth activation
  • Mortgage relationship → investable balance conversion
  • Card-only → funded Premier & wealth activation

Portfolio clean-up action plan

Remove structural dilution — without gaming the denominator

A · Reaffirm eligibility

  • Qualification rules
  • Grace periods
  • International / multi-market treatment
  • Upgrade & downgrade rules

B · Apply disciplined actions

  • Retain high-potential customers
  • Migrate to appropriate service models
  • Downgrade persistently unqualified
  • Protect credible recovery paths

C · Prevent re-entry

  • Stop upgrade–downgrade recycling (see CIIOM volatility)
  • Minimum quality thresholds on upgrades
  • Review origin & performance of upgrades
Guardrail — Clean-up supports portfolio health, but it cannot be the primary route to 50%. The denominator must fall because customers genuinely leave the segment, not to flatter the ratio.
