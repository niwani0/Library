## 1. Accountability Dysfunctions

### The Accountability Gap
**Signature**: Work falls through cracks. Finger-pointing. "I thought you were handling that."
**Root cause**: No clear owner, or owner lacks authority/resources
**Fix**: Assign explicit owner with authority. Make ownership visible.

### The Accountability Overlap
**Signature**: Conflict over territory. Duplicated work. "That's my area."
**Root cause**: Multiple people believe they own the same thing
**Fix**: Clarify boundaries. Split accountability or merge roles.

### Accountability Without Authority
**Signature**: Person held responsible but can't make decisions or allocate resources
**Root cause**: Responsibility assigned without corresponding power
**Fix**: Match authority to accountability. Or reduce accountability scope to match actual authority.

### Authority Without Accountability
**Signature**: Person makes decisions but isn't held to outcomes. "That's someone else's problem now."
**Root cause**: Decision maker insulated from consequences
**Fix**: Connect decision maker to outcomes. Feedback loops.

## 2. Coordination Dysfunctions

### The Handoff Problem
**Signature**: Quality drops at team boundaries. "Works on my machine." Blame shifting.
**Root cause**: Poor interface design between teams. No shared definition of done.
**Fix**: Define explicit contracts. Joint ownership of handoffs. Integration testing.

### The Meeting Tax
**Signature**: Calendar full of syncs. No time for actual work. Meeting about the meeting.
**Root cause**: Compensation for unclear roles, missing async channels, or trust deficit
**Fix**: Diagnose root cause. Often: clarify ownership, improve documentation, build trust.

### The Dependency Doom Loop
**Signature**: Team A waiting on Team B waiting on Team C. Everything blocked.
**Root cause**: Too many dependencies, insufficient capacity, no prioritization
**Fix**: Reduce dependencies (team self-sufficiency), increase capacity at bottleneck, or explicit prioritization mechanism.

### The Shadow Org
**Signature**: Formal structure doesn't match how work actually happens. Informal networks do the real coordination.
**Root cause**: Formal structure outdated or poorly designed
**Fix**: Either update formal structure to match reality, or investigate why people route around it.

## 3. Decision Dysfunctions

### Decision Paralysis
**Signature**: Decisions take forever. Analysis paralysis. "Let's get more data."
**Root cause**: Unclear who decides, fear of being wrong, misaligned incentives
**Fix**: Assign decision owner. Set time limits. Make decisions reversible where possible.

### The HiPPO
**Signature**: Highest Paid Person's Opinion always wins. Others stop contributing.
**Root cause**: Power dynamics override merit. Psychological safety deficit.
**Fix**: Structured decision processes. Explicit criteria. Leader speaks last.

### The Consensus Trap
**Signature**: Everyone must agree. Lowest common denominator outcomes. Endless debate.
**Root cause**: Conflict aversion. Unclear escalation paths.
**Fix**: Distinguish "consult" from "decide." One person accountable. Disagree and commit.

### Decision Revisiting
**Signature**: Same decision made repeatedly. Decisions reopened after the fact.
**Root cause**: Key stakeholders not consulted. Decision not documented. New information not distinguished from re-litigation.
**Fix**: RACI for decisions. Document decisions and rationale. Clear process for new information.

## 4. Growth Dysfunctions

### The Promotion Bottleneck
**Signature**: People stuck at level. Unclear how to advance. Departures at specific tenure.
**Root cause**: No growth paths, subjective promotion criteria, limited senior roles
**Fix**: Clear leveling rubrics. Development paths. Create senior opportunities.

### The Peter Principle Trap
**Signature**: Great ICs promoted to managers, then struggle. Performance drops after promotion.
**Root cause**: Promoting based on current role performance, not next role fit
**Fix**: Separate IC and management tracks. Evaluate for future role. Trial periods.

### Skill Atrophy
**Signature**: Managers can't do the work anymore. Technical debt rises. "Nobody knows how this works."
**Root cause**: Specialists promoted away from specialty, no knowledge transfer
**Fix**: Rotation, documentation, technical involvement for managers, succession planning.

## 5. Diagnostic Questions by Symptom

### "Everything is slow"
- Where do things wait? (Identify bottlenecks)
- Who has to approve? (Decision rights)
- What meetings are required? (Coordination overhead)
- What's the longest handoff? (Dependency chains)

### "Quality is dropping"
- Where does work change hands? (Handoff design)
- Who owns end-to-end quality? (Accountability)
- Are standards clear? (Expectations)
- Is there feedback to creators? (Loops)

### "People are frustrated"
- Can they do their job? (Resources/authority)
- Do they know what success looks like? (Clarity)
- Do they see a future here? (Growth)
- Are they heard? (Voice)

### "Teams don't collaborate"
- Do they have reason to? (Shared goals)
- Is it easy to? (Coordination mechanisms)
- Is there trust? (Relationship)
- Are there consequences for not collaborating? (Incentives)

### "We keep having the same problems"
- Do we know the root cause? (Diagnosis)
- Did we fix it or treat symptoms? (Intervention quality)
- Is the fix still in place? (Sustainability)
- Did conditions change? (Adaptation)
